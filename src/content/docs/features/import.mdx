<Steps>
  <Step title="Paste a URL">
    Any page that publishes a recipe — most cooking sites do — or a
    YouTube video link.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Recipery scrapes it">
    Title, ingredients, steps, servings, prep/cook time, cuisine, tags,
    and the hero photo, pulled from the page automatically.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and save">
    The imported recipe lands in your library like any other — edit
    anything before or after saving.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## How it works

- Recipery reads the [schema.org `Recipe`](https://schema.org/Recipe)
  JSON-LD most recipe sites already embed for Google's rich-results
  snippets — no site-specific scraper needed
- If the page has no `Recipe` JSON-LD, the import fails with an error
  rather than guessing
- The hero photo is downloaded and stored alongside the recipe; if the
  photo fails to download, the import still succeeds without one
- The imported recipe records its source (site or author name) and the
  original URL, shown on the recipe itself

<Aside type="tip" title="Identify your requests">
  Set `RECIPERY_CONTACT` (an email or contact URL) on the server — it's
  sent as part of the `User-Agent` on every import request, so sites
  seeing a lot of your imports have a way to reach you if something looks
  off. See the environment reference in
  [Deploy with Docker](/deployment/docker#environment-reference).
</Aside>

## Importing from YouTube

A YouTube link doesn't carry `Recipe` JSON-LD like a cooking site does,
so it's handled differently: Recipery pulls the title and channel name
from YouTube's oEmbed endpoint, grabs a clean thumbnail straight from the
video ID, and attaches the video to the recipe (see
[Video](/features/library#video)) — that part always works.

As a bonus, it also scans the video's description for a recipe, since
cooking channels often paste one in. It looks for lines shaped like an
ingredient ("500g flour", "1 tsp salt") and numbered steps ("1. Preheat
the oven"), skipping timestamps, links, and promotional text. When it
finds something, those become the recipe's ingredients and steps; when
it doesn't, they're left empty for you to fill in from the video. Either
way, double-check what comes in — description formatting varies a lot
from channel to channel.

Prefer typing a recipe in yourself, or a site doesn't publish structured
data? See [Library & Recipes](/features/library) for adding one by hand.

Don't have a URL in mind yet? [Discover & Find Similar](/features/discover)
searches recipe sites for you, right from the library.
